Our History

The Birth of The Boys' Brigade — Scotland, 1883

The Boys' Brigade was found by Sir Alexander Williams Smith. He was born in Glasgow, Scotland in October 27th, 1854.William A. Smith was an Army Officer before the lord called him for his work; he abandoned his military service and became a Sunday school Teacher at Free Church mission, Northwood side hall in Glasgow Scotland. It was found during the time parents left their children uncared for. Therefore children became wild, undisciplined and were not properly thought with the Bible, hence as an enthusiastic Sunday school Teacher, he discovered what was going on and applied his military knowledge forming what is called BOYS BRIGADE in order to see that children hears the word of God. Though this Boys Brigade organization started with 51 boys but due to some of the disobedience that attracts discipline actions for corrections which many of our companies are facing today made some of the boys ran away due to the hard (harsh) training in Brigade. So they remained only 31 boys including Sir William A. Smith and these were the people that made it to the first ever enrollment service in the Boys' Brigade. boys' Brigade started with 28 boys and 3 officers on 4th October 1883 at Northwood side hall Free Church mission Glasgow, Scotland. It was known as the 1st Glasgow Company in Scotland. The name of the 3 officers are John B. Hill, James R. Hill and Sir William A. Smith. The remaining 28 boys were shared into 3 sections: Junior Boys', Company Boys' and Senior Boys' . The Boys Brigade is the first Christian uniformed organization formed in the world. Sir William A. Smith became a knight in the year 1909. He died on 10th May 1914 at the age of 60 years after the work that God gave him to do.

What began with a single company grew into an international Christian youth movement. Its enduring motto, "Sure & Steadfast," reflects the commitment to faith, discipline and steadfastness that has guided generations of members.

The Arrival and Growth of Boys' Brigade Nigeria

The Boys' Brigade was introduced into Nigeria in 1908, with its earliest documented beginnings at Holy Trinity Anglican Church, Ebute Ero, Lagos. A. W. Wakeman, working with the church's leadership, helped establish the first company, beginning a journey that would spread the movement beyond its initial location.

As the organization expanded, the need for coordinated leadership and administration became increasingly important. In 1961, a historic meeting in Ilorin helped advance the formation of a unified national council. Subsequent organizational steps established the foundation for The Boys' Brigade Nigeria as a nationally coordinated movement.

Today, the Nigerian movement continues the founding vision through Christian education, drill, training, leadership development, fellowship and service to communities.

As part of The Boys' Brigade Nigeria, the Anambra State Council contributes to the continued growth and development of the Brigade through its companies, officers, battalions and other council structures across the state.

Through weekly company meetings, Boys' Brigade Week, training programmes, Founder's Day celebrations, camps, enrollment services, leadership programmes and competitions, the Council provides opportunities for members to strengthen their Christian faith, develop discipline, acquire knowledge and grow in leadership and service.

The Council remains committed to preserving the founding vision of the Brigade, supporting its officers, nurturing its members and preparing future generations to uphold the values of The Boys' Brigade.

"The Vision of our founders shall not die in our hands."

Every Company in the Council belongs to a local church, and every activity — from Sunday parade to summer camp — is led by trained volunteer officers who give their time freely so that the next generation can grow in faith, character, and skill.

The 4-Square Program Framework

Based on Luke 2:52 ("And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man"), the 4-Square program is a holistic training model designed to help young people grow balanced and well-rounded in body, mind, and spirit.

The Boys' Brigade seeks to develop the whole boy through four complementary areas of growth: Spiritual, Physical, Educational and Social. These programmes combine Christian education and discipline with practical, enjoyable and purposeful activities. Officers are encouraged to plan balanced programmes that reflect the interests, abilities and developmental needs of their boys, helping every member grow in faith, knowledge, character and service.

Spiritual

"In favour with God"

Building a personal relationship with Jesus Christ through Bible study, Bible stories, prayer, devotions, worship and Bible quiz competitions. Spiritual programmes should go beyond teaching alone by engaging boys through meaningful, enjoyable and interactive activities that help them understand God's Word, apply Christian values in everyday life, and grow into responsible individuals who positively influence their families and communities.

Physical

"In stature"

Developing fitness, self-discipline, confidence, teamwork and a spirit of comradeship through drill, football, volleyball, gymnastics, road walks, martial arts and other suitable physical activities. Drill encourages alertness, obedience, coordination and smartness, while outdoor activities help boys improve their fitness, posture, endurance and ability to work together. Activities should be age-appropriate, inclusive and safely supervised.

Educational

"In wisdom"

Broadening each boy's knowledge, interests, talents and aspirations through spelling and dictation, debates, quizzes, career talks, excursions, arts and crafts, practical skills and achievement badge activities. These programmes stimulate curiosity, strengthen learning beyond the classroom and help boys discover their abilities, set meaningful goals and prepare for future education, careers and responsible adulthood. Officers should plan activities according to the needs of their boys and the Company's annual programme.

Social (Community)

"In favour with man"

Nurturing good morals, citizenship, cooperation, respect and a sense of responsibility towards others. Through drama, excursions, debates, team activities, citizenship education and community service, boys learn to communicate effectively, work with others and contribute positively to society. Activities may include environmental clean-ups, charitable initiatives, church service and appropriate community safety-awareness projects, helping boys put Christian values into practical action.

Formation & Founder

A movement since 1883

Formation

The Boys' Brigade is the world's first uniformed youth organization, founded in Glasgow, Scotland, in 1883. It went on to inspire many of the uniformed youth movements that followed, and today operates across the Commonwealth and beyond, including a strong presence throughout Nigeria.

Our Founder

Sir William Alexander Smith (1854–1914) started The Boys' Brigade in 1883 and dedicated his life to it as Brigade Secretary. He was knighted by King Edward VII in 1909 for his work with young people. Founder's Day is marked each year on the last Sunday of October.

Training

The Officers & NCO'S pathway

A genuine progression — each stage builds on the one before it.

1

NCOs' COURSE (BASIC I-III)

Majorly held by the Battalion/Group Council annually, though the State Council may also conduct it. The course trains and equips boys with Boys' Brigade knowledge, basic drill, teamwork and discipline.

2

OFFICERS' COURSE (BASIC I-III)

Majorly held by the Battalion/Group Council annually, though the State Council may also conduct it. The course develops participants' Boys' Brigade knowledge, drill skills, teamwork and discipline.

3

OFFICERS' COURSE (BASIC IV)

Conducted by the State Council alone, biannually, to develop officers' advanced leadership and drill skills and prepare them for the National Brigade School.

4

NATIONAL BRIGADE SCHOOL COURSE

Conducted exclusively by the National Council on a biannual basis across Nigeria's six geographical regions.

5

NATIONAL TRAIN-THE-TRAINERS COURSE

Conducted exclusively by the National Council at three-year intervals.

6

ADVANCED NATIONAL TRAIN-THE-TRAINERS COURSE

Conducted exclusively by the National Council at five-year intervals.

EXAMINATION AND CERTIFICATION

All courses conclude with theory and drill examinations. Certificates are issued to participants who successfully pass the required examinations.
